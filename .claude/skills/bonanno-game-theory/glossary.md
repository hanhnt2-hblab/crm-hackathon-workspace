# Glossary — Bonanno, *Game Theory*

**AGM belief revision** — axiomatic theory of revising beliefs on *surprising* (probability-0) information; Alchourrón–Gärdenfors–Makinson 1985. Represented by a plausibility order (Ch 8, Ch 12)
**AGM-consistency** — an assessment whose supports match a plausibility order's plausibility-preserving actions (P1) and most-plausible histories (P2). Qualitative only (Ch 12)
**Agreement Theorem** — like-minded individuals cannot agree to disagree once the disagreement is common knowledge; Aumann 1976 (Ch 8)
**Arrow's Axiom** — `E ⊆ F` and `E ∩ f(F) ≠ ∅` ⟹ `f(E) = E ∩ f(F)`. Necessary but not sufficient for AGM (Ch 8)
**Assessment** — a pair `(σ, μ)`: behavioral strategy profile + system of beliefs (Ch 10)
**Backward induction** — mark terminal nodes, then repeatedly mark any decision node whose successors are all marked with the mover's best successor payoff (Ch 2)
**Backward-induction outcome** — the sequence of *actual* moves; distinct from the solution, which is a strategy profile (Ch 2)
**Bayes' rule** — `P(E|F) = P(F|E)P(E)/P(F)` (Ch 8)
**Bayes consistency** — for each equivalence class of `⪯` meeting `D_μ⁺`, a distribution `ν_E` satisfying B1–B3; the quantitative half of PBE (Ch 12)
**Bayesian Nash equilibrium** — Nash equilibrium of the game produced by the Harsanyi transformation. Nothing more than a Nash equilibrium (Ch 13)
**Bayesian updating** — modifying beliefs by the conditional probability rule; requires `P(E) > 0` (Ch 8, Ch 10)
**Behavioral strategy** — one probability distribution per information set, over the choices there (Ch 6)
**Best reply / best response** — `sᵢ` maximizing `πᵢ(·, s₋ᵢ)` (Ch 1)
**Cardinal utility** — synonym for vNM utility; unique up to positive affine transformation (Ch 4)
**Cardinal IDSDS** — iterated deletion of pure strategies strictly dominated by a **mixed** strategy; output = rationalizable strategies (Ch 5)
**Certainty** — assigning probability 1. **Can be false**, unlike knowledge (Ch 8)
**Chance move / Nature** — a fictitious player with an assigned probability distribution and no payoffs (Ch 3)
**Choice measurability** — a plausibility order has an integer representation `F` with `F(h) − F(h′) = F(ha) − F(h′a)`; a plausibility-distance-preserving condition (Ch 12)
**Common belief of rationality** — the belief-based (fallible) analogue of common knowledge of rationality; the author's preferred notion (Ch 9)
**Common knowledge** — everyone knows `E`, everyone knows that everyone knows `E`, ad infinitum. Computable via the common knowledge partition (Ch 7)
**Common knowledge partition** — `ℐ_CK(w)` = states **reachable** from `w`; obtained by merging overlapping information sets across all agents (Ch 7)
**Common prior** — a distribution `P` over states with `P(w′|ℐᵢ(w)) = P_{i,w}(w′)` for every `i`, `w` (Ch 8, Ch 13, Ch 15)
**Compound lottery** — a lottery whose prizes may themselves be lotteries; reduced by multiplying along paths and adding across them (Ch 4)
**Consistency (KW-consistency)** — an assessment is the limit of completely mixed strategy profiles and their Bayesian beliefs; Kreps–Wilson 1982 (Ch 11)
**Cooperative game theory** — players can communicate, form coalitions and sign **binding** agreements. Not covered by this book (Ch 0)
**Cournot competition** — quantity competition; Cournot invented what we now call Nash equilibrium (Ch 1, Ch 13)
**Decision node / terminal node** — outdegree > 0 / outdegree 0 (Ch 2)
**Degenerate lottery** — probability 1 on one basic outcome (Ch 4)
**DM (Decision-Maker)** — the individual whose preferences over lotteries are being represented (Ch 4)
**Dominant-strategy equilibrium** — every player's strategy is dominant. Unqualified, the default reading is **weak** (Ch 1)
**Event** — a subset of the state space; a proposition identified with its truth set (Ch 7)
**Expected utility** — `E[U(L)] = Σ pᵢU(oᵢ)` (Ch 4)
**Expected value** — `$(x₁p₁ + … + xₙpₙ)` for a money lottery (Ch 3)
**Extensive form (frame)** — tree + player assignment + action labels + outcomes; with information-set partitions in the general case (Ch 2, Ch 3)
**Full-support distribution** — assigns positive probability to every state (Ch 8, Ch 12)
**Game-frame** — a situation **without** preferences: `⟨I,(Sᵢ),O,f⟩`. Not a game (Ch 1)
**Harsanyi consistency** — synonym for like-mindedness: a common prior exists (Ch 8)
**Harsanyi transformation** — convert an incomplete-information situation into an extensive game: Nature picks the state, informed players observe, uninformed players do not (Ch 13)
**History** — a sequence of actions from the root; the node-naming device of Ch 12
**Homo rationalis** — Aumann's idealized agent: purposeful, logical, well-defined goals, sufficient calculating ability (Ch 0)
**Hotheaded type** — in the chain-store model, an incumbent who *enjoys* fighting (Ch 14)
**IDSDS** — iterated deletion of strictly dominated strategies. **Order-independent**; output ⟺ common belief of rationality (Ch 1, Ch 9)
**IDWDS** — iterated deletion of weakly dominated strategies. Order **matters**, so all dominated strategies must be deleted **simultaneously** at each step (Ch 1)
**Incomplete information** — at least one player does not know **what game she is playing** (Ch 13)
**Imperfect information** — at least one player may move without knowing others' earlier choices (Ch 3)
**Incredible threat** — a threatened action the threatener would not want to carry out when actually faced with the situation (Ch 2)
**Independent PBE** — a PBE rationalized by an order satisfying IND1 and IND2, with `μ` satisfying IND3 (Ch 12)
**Information partition** — a partition of the state space; its elements are information sets (Ch 7)
**Information set** — a collection of a player's decision nodes she cannot distinguish (Ch 3); a cell of her partition (Ch 7)
**Integer-valued representation** — `F: H → ℕ` with `F(h) ≤ F(h′) ⟺ h ⪯ h′`; **lower = more plausible** (Ch 12)
**Knowledge operator** — `KE = {w : ℐ(w) ⊆ E}`. Satisfies `KE ⊆ E`, `KKE = KE`, `K¬KE = ¬KE` (Ch 7)
**Like-mindedness** — see Harsanyi consistency (Ch 8)
**Losing position** — a state from which the mover cannot win while the opponent can (Ch 2)
**Lottery** — a probability distribution over basic outcomes; `ℒ(O)` is the set of them (Ch 4)
**Minimal proper subgame** — a proper subgame containing no other proper subgame; where the SPE algorithm starts (Ch 3)
**Mixed strategy** — a probability distribution over a player's **pure** strategies (Ch 5)
**Model of a game** — game + interactive knowledge-belief structure + strategy assignment `σᵢ: W → Sᵢ` (Ch 9)
**Money lottery** — a lottery whose outcomes are sums of money (Ch 3)
**Nash equilibrium** — every player's strategy is a best reply to the others' (Ch 1)
**Non-cooperative game theory** — binding agreements are impossible; the book's exclusive subject (Ch 0)
**Normalized utility function** — the vNM representative with `U(o_worst) = 0`, `U(o_best) = 1` (Ch 4)
**Ordinal utility function** — `U(o) > U(o′) ⟺ o ≻ o′`; the numbers are **arbitrary** beyond order (Ch 1)
**Pareto superior (strictly / weakly)** — every player prefers it / all weakly prefer and at least one strictly (Ch 1)
**Perfect Bayesian equilibrium (PBE)** — sequentially rational + rationalized by a plausibility order + Bayesian relative to it (Ch 12)
**Perfect information** — every player, when moving, knows all preceding moves; every information set is a singleton (Ch 2)
**Perfect recall** — a player always remembers what she knew and what she herself did (Ch 3). Hypothesis of Kuhn's theorem (Ch 6)
**Pivotal mechanism (Clarke mechanism)** — a public-goods game making truthful reporting weakly dominant (Ch 1)
**Plausibility order** — a complete, transitive relation on states (Ch 8) or a total pre-order on histories satisfying PL1–PL3 (Ch 12); **lower = more plausible**
**Pooling / separating equilibrium** — the informed player makes the **same** / **different** choices across her types (Ch 13)
**Proper subgame** — start at a non-root singleton-information-set node, take all successors; valid only if the oval **cuts no information set** (Ch 3)
**Pure strategy** — the ordinary notion of a strategy, once mixing is allowed (Ch 5)
**Rationalizable strategy** — one surviving cardinal IDSDS (Ch 5)
**Rationality at a state** — the chosen strategy maximizes expected payoff given the player's beliefs at that state (Ch 9)
**Reachability** — `w′` reachable from `w` if a chain of information sets (of **any** agents) links them; defines the CK partition (Ch 7)
**Reduced-form game** — the payoff-only representation; `O` and `f` are discarded (Ch 1)
**Risk averse / neutral / loving** — prefers `$E[L]` to `L` / indifferent / prefers `L`. Rationality does **not** dictate which (Ch 3, Ch 4)
**Second-price (Vickrey) auction** — highest bidder wins and pays the **second**-highest bid; truthful bidding is weakly dominant (Ch 1)
**Selten's Chain Store Game** — the *m*-town repeated entry game; backward induction fails to capture reputation (Ch 2), which incomplete information restores (Ch 14)
**Selfish and greedy** — cares only about own material payoff and prefers more to less. An **assumption**, never a default (Ch 1)
**Sequential equilibrium** — consistent (KW) + sequentially rational; Kreps–Wilson 1982. Refines subgame-perfect equilibrium (Ch 11)
**Sequential rationality** — at **every** information set, the player's strategy is optimal given her beliefs — allowing joint deviations at that set and her later ones (Ch 10)
**State** — a complete specification of the relevant facts about the world (Ch 7); in Part V, it also carries a game (Ch 13)
**Strategy** — a list of choices, one per decision node (Ch 2) or per information set (Ch 3)
**Strategy profile** — one strategy per player; `s = (s₁,…,sₙ) ∈ S` (Ch 1)
**Strict / weak dominance** — strictly better against every opponent profile / weakly better against all and strictly against at least one (Ch 1)
**Subgame-perfect equilibrium** — a Nash equilibrium whose restriction to every proper subgame is a Nash equilibrium of that subgame (Ch 3)
**Support** — the set of pure strategies (or states) assigned positive probability (Ch 5, Ch 8)
**System of beliefs `μ`** — a probability distribution over the nodes of each information set (Ch 10)
**Trembling-hand perfect equilibrium** — Selten 1975; strictly refines sequential equilibrium and **does** eliminate weakly dominated strategies. Outside the book's scope (Ch 11)
**True state** — the state that actually obtains. The book insists on designating it, noting the literature almost never does (Ch 13)
**Type** — a utility function **plus** beliefs about others' types; corresponds to a cell of the player's information partition (Ch 15)
**Uniform Bayesian consistency** — existence of a full-support common prior satisfying UB1 and UB2 (Ch 12)
**Von Neumann-Morgenstern ranking** — a ranking of `ℒ(O)` satisfying the four Expected Utility Axioms: Completeness/transitivity, Monotonicity, Continuity, Independence (Ch 4)
**Weak sequential equilibrium** — sequentially rational + Bayesian updating **at reached information sets only**. Refines Nash but **not** subgame-perfect equilibrium (Ch 10)
**Win-lose game** — two outcomes, "1 wins" and "2 wins"; one player always has a winning strategy (Ch 2)
**Winning strategy** — guarantees a win regardless of the opponent's play (Ch 2)
